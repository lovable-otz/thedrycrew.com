Burst Pipes and Hidden Leaks

The First Hour

The Leak You Cannot See

The expensive version of this job is not the dramatic burst. It is the slow leak behind a wall or under a slab that nobody sees for months, because gradual damage is where policies stop paying.

The signs worth acting on are a water bill that moves without a change in use, a warm patch on a slab floor, staining that returns after painting, and a musty smell that is stronger in one room. Any of those is worth a moisture reading before it becomes a remediation.

Frequently asked questions

What do I do first when a pipe bursts?

Shut the water off at the main, cut power to affected circuits if water has reached outlets or a ceiling, then photograph everything before you move anything. Call for extraction after that, not before the water is off.

Is a burst pipe covered by insurance?

Usually, because it is sudden and accidental. A slow leak that has been running behind a wall for months is generally treated as gradual damage and excluded.

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

A water bill that rises without a change in use, a warm spot on a slab floor, staining that comes back after painting, or a musty smell concentrated in one room. Any of those justifies a moisture reading.

How do I find a leak I cannot see?

A water bill that rises without a change in use, a warm patch on a slab floor, staining that returns after painting, or a musty smell concentrated in one room. Any one of those justifies a moisture reading.

Are frozen pipes covered?

Usually, if you took reasonable care to keep the property heated. Leaving a house unheated in winter is a common basis for reduction, so check the wording before a cold snap rather than after.

What is a slab leak?

A leak in the supply line running under a concrete floor slab. It is expensive because access means cutting the slab, and it often shows up as a warm floor or an unexplained water bill before any water appears.

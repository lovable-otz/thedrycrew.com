What Water Damage Restoration Costs

The Published Ranges

Most restoration firms will not put a number on a page. Here are the ranges, with the source and the date attached.

JobTypical US range
Water damage restoration, whole claim$1,300 to $5,900
Clean water (Category 1), drying without demolition$3.75 to $7 per sq ft
Gray water (Category 2), partial drywall and flooring removal$7 to $12 per sq ft
Black water (Category 3), full demolition and structural drying$12 to $25+ per sq ft
Mold remediation, contained area$1,100 to $3,400

Source: Angi and operator-published rate cards compiled August 2026. Ranges are national and a range is never a quote.

What Actually Drives the Number

Area matters less than people expect. Category matters more.

  • Water category: clean, gray or black, which can triple the rate per square foot
  • Time before extraction started, because category degrades as water stands
  • Whether materials dry in place or have to be removed and replaced
  • Equipment days: air movers and dehumidifiers are usually billed per unit per day
  • Call-out timing, since nights, weekends and storm surges carry premiums

What People Get Wrong

The common mistake is comparing two quotes on price alone without checking what category each one assumes. A job priced as Category 1 on water that is really Category 2 will be revised upward once the work starts, and by then the equipment is in the house and the leverage has moved.

The second mistake is treating the deductible as the whole exposure. Insurers pay against their own schedules, so the gap between a retail quote and what a policy pays is a real number worth asking about before work begins, not after.

Frequently asked questions

How much does water damage restoration cost?

Between $1,300 and $5,900 for a typical claim. By area, $3.75 to $7 per square foot for clean water dried without demolition, rising to $12 to $25 or more per square foot for Category 3 work where contaminated materials are removed rather than cleaned.

Why is sewage cleanup so much more expensive?

Because the work changes from drying to demolition. Porous materials that have been in contact with Category 3 water are removed and replaced rather than cleaned and dried, so the labour and disposal costs rise with the materials.

Does the price include the repair?

Usually not. Restoration covers extraction, drying and removal of unsalvageable material. Rebuilding drywall, flooring and cabinetry is separate work, often by a separate trade, and it is worth getting that scope priced at the same time.

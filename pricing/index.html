What Water Damage Work Actually Costs

The Ranges

Clean water dries at $3.75 to $7 per square foot, gray water costs $7 to $12, and Category 3 runs $12 to $25 or more because materials are removed rather than dried. A whole claim typically lands between $1,300 and $5,900, and mold remediation of a contained area adds $1,100 to $3,400.

Every figure here carries its source and the date it was checked. Ranges are national; local pricing varies with labour rates and how far a crew has to travel.

What Moves a Quote

Four things account for most of the variation between two quotes on the same house.

  • Water category, which can triple the per-square-foot rate on its own
  • How long the water stood before extraction started
  • Whether materials can be dried in place or must be removed and replaced
  • Emergency call-out timing: nights, weekends and storm surges carry premiums

What to Ask Before Signing

Ask for the water category in writing before you sign a work authorisation. Category is what sets the price, and a scope that does not name it can be revised upward later without ever contradicting itself.

Ask whether the drying target is a moisture reading or a number of days. Ask who issues the clearance test if mold is involved, and whether that firm is the same one doing the remediation - in several states it legally cannot be.

Frequently asked questions

Why do restoration companies hide their prices?

Because pricing depends on the water category and the affected area, both of which need a visit to establish. That is a fair reason for a range rather than a fixed number, but it is not a reason to publish nothing, which is what most of the industry does.

Is a cheap quote a bad sign?

Not on its own, but check what category it assumes. A quote priced as Category 1 on water that is actually Category 2 will be revised upward once work starts, and by then the equipment is in your house.

Does insurance pay these rates?

Insurers pay against their own schedules, commonly Xactimate pricing. A crew that bills insurance directly is quoting against that schedule rather than the retail ranges above.

Are these prices what insurance pays?

Not directly. Insurers pay against their own schedules, commonly Xactimate pricing, so a crew billing insurance is quoting against that rather than these retail ranges.

Why publish ranges at all if every job differs?

Because a range you can check beats a number you cannot. Most of the industry publishes nothing, which leaves a homeowner with no way to tell a fair quote from an opportunistic one.

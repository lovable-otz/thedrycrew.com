Storm and Flood Damage

Two Claims One Storm

One storm can produce two claims. Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof is usually a homeowners claim; rising water across the threshold is a flood claim needing separate cover. Floodwater is Category 3 by definition, so it runs $12 to $25 or more per square foot. Photograph the entry point before anything is repaired.

Water that comes through a roof or a broken window in wind-driven rain is usually a homeowners claim. Water that comes in across the threshold from outside is usually a flood claim, on a separate policy. The same storm can produce both in the same house.

They are documented differently and adjusted by different people, which is why the point of entry needs photographing before anything is cleaned up.

The Documentation That Pays

Storm losses are the ones where evidence disappears fastest, because the instinct is to clear up.

  • Photograph the point of entry before covering or repairing it
  • Photograph the water line on walls before extraction
  • Keep a dated log of when the water arrived and how fast it rose
  • Keep receipts for tarping, boarding and temporary accommodation
  • Do not dispose of damaged material until it has been seen or photographed in place

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover storm flooding?

Wind-driven rain entering through a damaged roof or window is usually covered. Rising water entering from outside is excluded and needs separate NFIP or private flood cover, which carries a waiting period.

What should I photograph first?

The point of entry, before you cover or repair it, and the water line on the walls before extraction starts. Those two images do more for a claim than anything taken afterwards.

Can I get flood insurance after a storm is forecast?

No. NFIP policies carry a waiting period specifically to prevent that, so cover has to be in place well before a named storm.

Why do I need flood insurance if I am not in a flood zone?

Because a flood zone map describes where a lender requires cover, not where water goes. A large share of flooded homes in major US events sat outside the mapped 100-year floodplain.

How long is the NFIP waiting period?

There is one, which is the point: cover cannot be arranged once a storm is named and forecast. Confirm the current period with NFIP or your carrier before hurricane season rather than during it.

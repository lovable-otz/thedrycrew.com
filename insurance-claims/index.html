Water Damage Insurance Claims

The Line That Decides the Claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line, is usually covered. Gradual leaks generally are not, and rising water from outside is excluded entirely, needing separate NFIP or private flood cover with a waiting period. Mitigation is a policy obligation, so waiting for the adjuster before extraction can reduce a payout.

Standard homeowners policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental. A burst supply line at two in the morning is the textbook case. What they generally exclude is gradual damage, and what they exclude outright is flood, meaning water that rises and enters from outside.

That single distinction settles most claims, and it turns on where the water came from rather than how much of it there was. The same storm can produce a covered roof leak and an excluded ground-water entry in the same house, documented differently.

Flood is a Separate Policy

Flood damage needs NFIP or private flood cover, bought separately from the homeowners policy. NFIP policies carry a waiting period, so cover cannot be arranged once a storm is named and forecast.

Being outside a mapped flood zone means a lender did not require the cover. It has never meant the property does not flood.

What Protects a Claim

Mitigation is a policy obligation, not a courtesy. Waiting for an adjuster before starting extraction usually increases the loss and can reduce what is paid.

  • Photograph everything before anything is moved or removed
  • Get the water category stated in writing on day one
  • Keep moisture logs and the equipment record, not just the invoice
  • Keep damaged materials where practical until the adjuster has seen them
  • Record who you spoke to and when, on every call

Frequently asked questions

Is a burst pipe covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually yes, because it is sudden and accidental. A pipe that has been weeping behind a wall for months is normally treated as gradual damage and excluded, which is why the age of the leak becomes the argument.

Should I wait for the adjuster before starting work?

No. Policies require you to mitigate further damage, and delay usually increases the loss rather than protecting the claim. Photograph everything first, then let extraction start.

Do I need separate flood insurance?

For rising water, yes. It is excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs NFIP or private flood cover. That cover carries a waiting period, so it has to be in place before a storm is forecast, not after.

Can a claim be denied for waiting too long?

It can be reduced. Mitigation is a policy obligation, so damage that got worse while you waited for an adjuster may not be covered even when the original loss was.

Should I hire a public adjuster?

It depends on the size and the dispute. A public adjuster works for you rather than the insurer and takes a percentage, so it is worth considering on a large or contested claim and rarely on a small clean one.

What if the payout does not cover the quote?

Ask for the insurer's line-item scope and compare it against your contractor's. Gaps are usually specific items rather than a general shortfall, and specifics are what a supplement is built from.

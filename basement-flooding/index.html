Flooded Basement Cleanup

The Houston Picture

Flooded basement cleanup runs $12 to $25 or more per square foot when floodwater is involved, because that is Category 3 and the materials come out rather than being dried. Standard homeowners policies exclude rising water entirely. Pump-out should start the same day, since standing water degrades by the hour.

The Insurance Split

This is where homeowners are most often caught out. Standard homeowners cover excludes rising water. Flood damage needs NFIP or private flood cover, bought separately, and NFIP policies carry a waiting period, so it cannot be arranged once a storm is named.

Water that comes through the roof in wind-driven rain is usually a homeowners claim. Water that comes in across the threshold is usually a flood claim. The same storm can produce both in the same house, and they are documented differently.

Permits for the Repair

Structural repair after flooding is permitted through the Permitting Center, and floodplain rules can apply to the reconstruction itself, not only to new builds. If the property is in a regulated floodplain, substantial-improvement rules may govern how it can be rebuilt.

Check before demolition rather than after, because the answer can change what is worth removing.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Generally not. Rising water is excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate NFIP or private flood cover. Given 68% of the homes flooded in Harvey sat outside the mapped floodplain, being told you do not need it is worth verifying.

How much does flooded home cleanup cost?

Floodwater is Category 3, so it sits at the top of the range: $12 to $25 or more per square foot, because contaminated porous materials are removed rather than dried. A typical US restoration job overall runs $1,067 to $5,169.

Do I need a permit to repair flood damage?

Structural work is permitted through the US Permitting Center, and properties in a regulated floodplain can face additional substantial-improvement requirements. Ask before demolition, because the rules can change what gets removed.

Will a sump pump stop this happening again?

It helps with ground water, not with a blocked drain or a burst supply line. Fixing the recurrence means identifying which of those caused it, which is what the first visit should establish.

Is a flooded basement covered by insurance?

It depends entirely on where the water came from. A burst pipe is usually covered; ground water rising through the floor is a flood exclusion needing separate NFIP or private cover.

Can I pump it out myself?

For clean water, sometimes. For anything that came in from outside, no: that is Category 3, carrying pathogens, and it needs containment, protective equipment and a disposal route.
